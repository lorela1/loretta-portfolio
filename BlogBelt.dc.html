WEEK 1 September 24th 2025

Astral Automaton Assistance -- A Space Robot Piloting Game. The full pitch deck can be accessed here: https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1la7jtDXJUL7M4V22Op3Z0oAOmcCT703znD92CZyJzyw/edit?usp=sharing
Knowing that I wanted to create a winning game pitch for this project, I came to the first team meeting prepared. I thought carefully and strategically about the kind of idea that would be appropriate for a ten week project -- the idea had to be something easy to understand because anything too unique, niche or esoteric would require too much detailed communication to fit into a 3 week pre-production planning period, appropriately scoped for a team of between 15 -- 20 developers spread across two campuses. It had to be an idea that people understood without having to make everyone read a 100 page design document about, if that makes sense.

I also took into account the composition of our team, as I it was absolutely key to the brief that everyone have their own 'design domain' so to speak -- with 6 designers in total the 'creative vision' would have to be split 6 ways and that is not an easy task.

Astral Automaton Assistance (pardon the pun, the team thought it was fun) is a game about being a scrap-collecting robot in space. The primary game loop would be a resource management loop, which I would have assigned to team member KS to design, as that is his passion. I know that team member MB has a good eye for 'cosy' games and would probably be keen on putting her design mind toward the crafting side of the game. And I also know that GK would be great on UI design. Even with those areas covered I still thought the idea had a lot of space for creative input by designers on the Birmingham office -- narrative, worldbuilding and combat design would still be up for grabs. These were my reasons for choosing this idea -- that 'space' is a setting that is easy to grasp without a huge need for lots of laboured explaination, and that it was an idea that was complex enough to allow lots of scope for creative input by a designer-heavy team.

I lead the Guildford based team members on a design journey where we all contributed ideas to Astral Automaton Assistance using a shared Miro board.

It is important to the success of any game development project that the members all have a certain basic level of buy-in to the idea. I think the simplest way to enhance team buy-in is to allow all members to give their opinions about the overall design of the game openly, in a collaborative and democratic way, at least in the beginning stages of ideation before things have to be set in stone for the production process. This is the part of game development that I enjoy the most, the part where a group of creative minds can come together and discuss ideas without any huge stakes involved (yet!). The members of the Guildford office seemed keen on Astral Automaton Assistance, possibly because all of us have played at least one space-related game in our lives. That was when I knew I had hit upon the right theme for my pitch!

As it happened we were asked by the Official Project Leadership to flesh out our pitch with more details as well as to allow time for the Birmingham-based part of the team to come up with their own pitch to show the Guildford devs. I thought that was fair enough.

WEEK 2 October 1st 2025

Sinner's Belt pitch slide on their proposed art style. I don't really see what exactly is marrying all these very disparate images together, but then AAA games aren't known to have very unique art styles anyway.
Today we heard the pitch of the game idea Sinner's Belt from the two designers on the Birmingham side of the project. It's an FPS set in a dystopian spacefaring future. Which seems perfectly fine as an idea. At the end of the day it doesn't truly matter which idea gets made, mainly that a game that we can all be proud of gets made.

Problems in our design collaboration started to make themselves clear during the discussion between the Guildford and Birmingham devs on which game to choose to make in our remaining 9 weeks of development time. It was clear that the communication style of the desingers IB and RA was causing a great deal of friction. One of the things that I have learned during my time as a game developer in the past few years is that the success of any project is almost entirely down to communication -- both in style and content. People who work together have to empathise with each other, need to understand and listen with thoughtfulness and sometimes sensitivity.

This was not the case in the way that IB and RA were giving feedback to our pitch, and their dismissive tone put everyone on edge in a way that was enormously counterproductive. The meeting was needlessly contentious. By the end of the discussion people were worn down and tired of the infighting, which was fair enough. And so people voted to make Sinner's Belt, simply to end the debate, which I thought was a poor choice given how hard FPS games are to make to an impressive standard, and how saturated the FPS game market is at every single level. As a designer I was worried about making the game look and feel polished, as well as to stand out amongst the crowd, but on balance AAA games -- and it was a key part of the brief that we needed to make a AAA-like game -- are hardly known for their uniqueness anyway.

At the end of the meeting I was appointed Lead Designer, which I took to mean that I made most of the production critical design-related decisions, and KS was appointed Head of Production, which means that he will be making resource-allocation decisions and keeping the project on schedule. Delivering on the final product in a timely and cost-effective manner is the holy grail of all AAA production and KS and I were keen to make that a reality in the next 9 weeks' time.

WEEK 3 October 8th 2025

This week was spent pre-production. Designers IB and RA were required to come up with a Worldbuilding design document and a Combat Design document respectively. After having read and interpreted IB's Worldbuilding document I set about writing a Quest and Level Design document and doing the legwork that would communicate my vision to the rest of the team. I convened a meeting with the team's environment artists to discuss the scope of the level we were to produce within the 10 week timespan of this project.

As part of my role as designer I convened a separate smaller meeting without the programmers about beginning the level block-out and visual character design and ideation. I included combat desingner RA as I thought her input would be valued. However as it transpired RA had some rigid ideas about what should or shouldn't be in the quest, which I thought was my domain of design. Her excessive stubborness about certain details of how the level should be designed, which I had understood was my area of work, and her insistence that we should down-scope the project to her preference, despite not having discussed this with our production manager KS who is actually the person with the final say, was truly baffling.

In the event, things came to a fractious head during our weekly All-Hands meeting, in which RA and IB called for my resignation as Lead Designer. I was aware of RA's hostility towards me and my ideas but I'm not sure this was a move that necessarily accomplished very much in terms of moving the project forward. At the end of the day each designer on the team, myself included, still MUST to have the 'freedom' to design their portion of the game, as according the project brief. My ideas and design have to make it in to the level design of the game no matter who is Lead Designer -- which in my humble opinion is a design flaw of the brief itself. In other words, I think the design brief of the AAA Development Project itself is flawed because in a real studio environment there would be no vying for power in this way. People would have their assigned roles and stay within them.

The major lesson of this whole demoralising fiasco is that communication is everything. We should have spent much more time defining the design space of each design team member. We should have made more time to speak to one another to clarify our roles.

I was asked to redesign some part of the quest by IB which I felt was a lot of wasted effort on my part since his Worldbuilding document was so sparse that it caused MB and myself to design in the wrong direction, so to speak. At any rate at least the leadership issues are behind us, and we can progress at pace with production, which should begin this week.

WEEK 4 October 15th 2025

I get the sense that the artists on our team were getting worried about the direction of the project. So I spoke to them about my quest and level design, as well as providing them with a written document to refer to, in order to guide their concepting of enemies and environment art. Here, again RA became a blocker in the production process. She disagreed again with my quest design and tried to essentially bully me into changing it, which is not actually a thing that she has the power to do, according to our brief.

The core issue was that the Combat Design document that RA had produced was not helpful in supporting MB and myself in our roles as Quest and Level designers. The Combat Design document was not the clearest and RA herself did not show the kind of leadership needed for a person in her role -- the design of the combat shapes the design of the level, and RA made no attempt to initiate communication to MB and myself about her ideas, other than to send us her 'work in progress' combat design document.

The over reliance on design documents to communicate might be one of the critical issues in the blockers hampering production. The level of distrust and disunity has lead the designers from the Birmingham office (RA and IB) to communicate only via design documents -- which I think is flawed. Ideally the design document should be a tool in the many communication tools that we have and not the *last word* or somehow an End Goal in the game design process. The design documents should be supporting our creative process, not hampering it. It was a case of the tail wagging the dog rather than the other way around, so to speak. The Agile process emphasises communcation over documentation, and this is a prime example of how the leadership in the Birmingham office failed to adhere to Agile teamwork methods.

It also seemed to me that I was being asked to do the impossible during this week's All Hands meeting in which IB could not see that writing the dialogue of the game characters must needs be a part of the Quest Designer's role. Designing a quest includes designing the characters within the quest, and so we were circling back to the root of all the problems we have had in this production -- that the brief itself asked us to share power equally and we somehow had not built enough trust and tolerance of each other to do it. To be fair, given the bullying I have been recieving I felt my distrust was justified.

All of this became a moot point as the Guildford and Birmingham team members were asked to split and each go their own way, to produce two versions of Sinner's Belt. I think everyone breathed a sigh of relief when this was announced, as it meant an end to the constant infighting within Design.

Looking back on it all, I do worry that I had a part in the excessive distrust between IB and RA on the Birmingham side, and myself and MB in the Guildford office. I asked for feedback from the official project leader VS and he pointed out that occaisionally during meetings I speak in a way that does not make absolutely clear that I have fully taken in and considered someone else's viewpoint. I hope to be a profilic (if not successful -- that's never gauranteed!) game designer and therefore team leader in the future, and what VS said as an outside observer did give me some food for thought. I think I must learn to become a better leader by not just listening and considering, but showing that I have listened, considered and appreciated my fellow team members. Communication IS EVERYTHING in collaboration. Every. Thing. Because at the heart of the game production process there are PEOPLE.

Also asking for feedback. Because it is important to iterated on your processes -- to learn and improve. It's at the heart of the Sprint logs that we write each week, that we should look back and apply lessons forward.

WEEK 5 October 22nd 2025

This week's All Hands meeting was met with a palpable sense of relief at the severing of ties with designers IB and RA from the Birmingham office. The morale of the whole team was lifted at the prospect of not having to second guess what the leadership in the Birmngham office where going to come up with as fresh new blockers to the production process. MB the level designer was able to dive in freshly energised at her task of designing the leve, ZZ the art lead was able forge ahead with defining the art style of the project with fresh aplomb. Not much has changed for the programming team as the Guildford Office is home to the majority of the programmers anyway, but the UI implementation work was reassigned to MG

I spent the week proslytising my Quest and Level design document. As reinstated Lead Designer I was excited by the opportunity to write the dialogue for the scene. Sadly our previous production manager KS has dropped out of the team and I therefore had to shoulder some of his responsibilities as production manager, but GK and I decided to share out the tasks that were left behind in his wake and I was grateful for her support on that.

GK and I have started keeping fresh sprint logs ( available to view here https://docs.google.com/document/d/1dJXxxVeM81jkG_0aSXVE7bANn4Z6HtlncJbmluu6fNM/edit?usp=sharing ) -- the first 4 weeks are missing as that was KS' job but he is out of contact and missing in action.

The biggest blocker for the project this week has been the artist EU's lack of confidence in the area of level blockouts. He and I ( designer MB has recently been hampered by illness so I am attempting to fill in her role in the meantime) are supposed to be working together on the level design blockout since he has finished modelling the 'liquid ammo' gun that is held by the player character. I feel that his reluctance to learn things on his own by researching the task is not something I can necessarily 'fix' as a leader. I tried to give him as much support as possible by reaching out and asking our previous environment artist in the Birminham office AD to give him a set of modular 3D 'pieces' to use to build an environment out of that AD had already prepared. We shall just have to wait and see if EU will rise to the occaision and see this as the learning opportunity that it is.

In terms of iterating on the collaborative nature of game development, and improving on teamwork week by week (as is the Agile way!) on the whole I have decided to wholly commit to over-communicating and being much more present on discord for my team as Lead Designer than before. Use of the project management software Codecks is still a huge problem as people seem to see it as a nuisance admin task rather than as a tool, which I have some sympathy with. It has much more utility for the production team (myself and GK) than for each individual team member, who must concern themselves only with the smaller scope of their own individual task. I'm not sure how to solve this problem of Lack Of Kanban Usage but we will bring it up as often as possible to the team.

WEEK 6 29th October 2025

The team has made some great progress this week, especially GK on the UI design. Since there was very little spare artist capacity GK made all the UI assets herself.

In terms of my workload I've switched over from wearing my Designer hat to wearing my Production Manager hat, reminding the programming team that we need to be looking at a first playable in the next week as a matter of urgency. Our last All Hands sprint meeting is set for Nov 26th which is merely a month away and we are all anxious to finish the game's 'vertical slice' in time for playtesting and bug fixing.

In terms of Codecks useage an unexpected communication problem cropped up.

When we went through how to use Codecks together as a team at the beginning of the process, around Week 1, and I thought the answer to this question was so obvious it didn't bear mentioning, but of course it goes to show that I am still learning about collaborating multidisciplinary teams making complex projects under time pressure. Say the unsaid. Say everything twice if not three times. Because when in any doubt, people will just neglect to adopt the tool into their workflow, even if intellectually they can understand that good project management is in fact good for the project as a whole, and therefore a common good for everyone on the team.

WEEK 7 November 5th 2025

I was impressed by the leadship of Lead Programmer EW in pulling together a first playable for us this week. I won't post any pictures or videos of it because without the art implemented it's actually not much to see. The design feedback that we gave to the programmers is that they have to think more towards giving the game more 'game juice' and feedback in the way that the player character is controlled.

Artist EU struggled with completing the game's splash screen design to the team's satisfaction this week. On the whole MB's communication style was not helpful as I believe she uses much harsher language than really needed, although I'm not entirely sure she's fully aware of her communication style issues.

It's unfortunate but artist EU was unable to grasp the kind of realistic 'AAA look' we were going for -- we tried our best to communicate to him what was needed, tried to guide him to read the Art Style Guide as written by art lead ZZ but to no avail. I think this simplified , bold 2D look could be brilliant in another type of game. There are no bad artists, I think, just that art needs the right context and this was not the right context for EU's art.

I am as Lead Designer rather concerned about the general lack of progress on all of the art that is needed to be done for the whole project. I got the sense that all the more accomplished, practiced artists were in the Birmingham office and the artists in the Guildford office were both fewer in number and earlier in their digital game art journey. So we (designer MB and I ) reiterated that the 'Art Implemented Playable' deadline -- this is our name for a version of the game will all the assets in place -- was in only one week's time.

On a personal level I am feeling a bit of mid-project slump. This is probably normal, as we're at the part of the process where nothing looks nice (no art in place) and everything is stilll janky feeling gameplay-wise (many bugs that need to be fixed in the first playable.) This, however, I know shall pass. As soon as the art and animations get put in place, team morale is usually enormously boosted because the project then feels much more accomplished and real.

WEEK 8 November 12th 2025

With the end of the project only a fortnight away the whole team is very stressed and anxious. As production managers GK and I had to but the (mild, metaphorical) thumbscrews on the Art Team who have not delivered, at all, on their committment to having all the art assets completed in implemented by the time of the All Hands meeting mid-week.

MB has taken on the task of making the game's menu/splash page, as we deemed that the energy we spent trying to 'manage' artist EU into doing what was needed to fit the brief too cumbersome, and MB could get it done relatively quickly anyway. The same could be said for all the in-game environment props on our asset list. ZZ the art lead agreed with us that this was the most expedient way forward was to just use ready made, free to download assets to populate the scene, instead of trying too hard to work with EU who doesn't seem to have a feel for the kind of look we need for this project. Ultimately the project comes first, above any individual person's ego. So we had to take away some art tasks from EU but mindful of the conflict-ridden first half of this project, I made a note of thanking EU for his contribution in modelling the player character's gun, which honestly is quite a professional looking well-done job.

A example of art by level designer MB who was the one who ended up creating the game splash page, to be honest the workflow was very much veering into indie dev territory where everyone just pitches in and does what is needed!
In the event we gave the Art Team another half a sprint's time to complete the tasks to fulfill the Art Playable requirement. I'm certain this will have knock-on effects on Sound Design and Playtesting, but we're in a bind and have no choice, given the time wasted on Design Team conflicts in the first 4/5 weeks of the project. I'm almost certain that the dialogue implementation will be cut, but I've written the dialogue already so we went ahead and made the voice recording sound assets anyway, just in case a miracle happens and sound designer CM has spare time to put it into the game.

Render of an enemy model by artist WW -- impressive!

By the end of the week the art team were showing us some of the amazing modelling they were doing for the enemy NPCs and of course the stress and anxiety all seemed worth it.

Codecks use is still abyssmal and GK and I have nagged the team about it endlessly -- nothing more can be done at this point from a leadership perspective. Or at least I can't think of anything that will incentivise the team to use Codecks more other than to remind them (which we have!) that it is mission-critical for them to use AAA production managment tools as part of this project.

WEEK 9 19th November 2025

During our All Hands this week I thought it my duty as Lead Designer to bring up the fact that the game is still quite short on gameplay content, as the project brief stated that it needed to contain 10 minutes of gameplay. We had a great meeting where everyone ( we're all gamers) pitched in and brainstormed different ideas for how to make the game last longer whilst continuing to only use the tools and assets that we already have. It was one of those gamedev moments that I live for -- morale was high because the prototype is looking and playing rather well, and we all had some amount of breathing space to just be together as a group of creatives who all love games, bouncing ideas off each other about game design.

The production leadership instituted a hard stop this week on all non-sound related production. The finish line seems very near, but in some ways this is only the beginning of the end. There's still bug fixing and playtesting, which will inevitably bring up some terrible game-breaking bugs. We're braced for impact however -- I've honestly enjoyed working with this team, and believe that the vertical slice we've produced is some of the best, most professional looking, polished game dev work I've ever done.

Screenshot of our game. I love the glow effect on the UI element at the bottom of the screen.

In terms of blockers MB and ZZ had a disagreement about the texturing of the environment walls. I had to step in as Lead Designer to artbitrate but it was solved very quickly with minimal conflict, as they recognised that I had final say in the matter, and was willing to go with my final call. I think it was a good example of how having a well-set-up team heirarchy does help with the smooth running of the project -- AAA game production must rely on this kind of personnel heirarchy in order to ensure the project isn't ensnared by uncertainty and conflict. Something to remember and learn from for future projects, that while heirarchies of decision making power seem quite harsh, when there is none and the team is big, the time wasted in conflict can outweigh the benefits of a more democratic and collegial team atmosphere.

WEEK 10 26th November 2026

This was an incredibly hectic week for our team as we scrambled to cram in all the things that we would have had ample time for had the project started on time and not been affected by the split we suffered at week 4.

I lead a group internal playtesting session in which we tweaked the parameters of the game to fit more closely to the design brief. I marveled at the gamefeel and gameplay that the programmers were able to produce in a short 10 weeks. I guided the adjusting of the game parameters until the game felt challenging but not impossible. (We also did a great deal of bug reporting and fixing, which is of course less gratifying but incredibly important to the QA process)

In the days after our final All Hands in-person sprint meeting I also took the lead in creating a playtesting survey (and compiled the data for that here: https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1y9Zvyl_rUh-VndlUJ6uRti8JzMiC-roYNw82HmUT2xc/edit?gid=2044264005#gid=2044264005 ) I receivced criticism from WW for using the phrase 'mise en scene' in the survey which was deemed too esoteric for a survey that was supposed to be able to be parsed by most people, so I adjusted it by adding a definition of 'mise en scene' to the survey to remedy that.

On the whole people who playtested the game compared the game favourably to the type of AAA FPS that we had in mind when we (the design team) were designing, so I am overall pleased with the end product.

Designer MB was especially gratified by this playtester's comment

There was some negative feedback around the prototype being lacking in story context. I reasoned that, had Sound Designer CM had more time to implement the HUD agent dialogue a lot of the story context would have been much more clear to the players. We had to cut the sound implementation time quite significantly due to the failure of the Art Team to deliver all their art according to agreed deadlines. This had a huge knock on effect on both the quality of the sound implementation, and the quality of the game as a whole.

The final game environment
I think my proudest contribution to the game is the design of a mini-tutorial area at the beginning of the game (which can be found at this link to itch.io https://loretta-lok.itch.io/sinners-belt). My design was able to onboard all the playtesters successfully, which is exactly what I had aimed for as the quest and level designer for this particular vertical game slice.

UI Designer/Artist GK and I worked together on the design and implentation of this 'quick and dirty' tutorial image popup at the beginning of the demo as part of the game's player onboarding. It worked well, and I was pleased!

WEEK 11 December 3rd 2025

Postmortem

The game logo designed by GK -- I love that it incorporates the 'Tristellaris' worldbuilding idea that I wrote into the Game Design Document.

The whole team had a chance to discuss and register their views on how the running of the project accorded with their expectations. On the whole the post mortem process, lead by MB, did not throw up any big surprises.

Everyone thought that the conflict that we had within the design team had a huge knock-on effect on the quality of the game we were as a team able to produce. Again, I think the lesson learned here is that areas of responsibility within roles should be dicussed in much more detail, and that a design team should try to build trust between each other as much as possible by expressing empathy and sensitivity in the way they communicate to each other. It all seems very obvious in hindsight, but it cost us 4 weeks of development time.

The lack of clarity in roles -- where one role ends and where another begins -- was also an issue when it came to management of work within the Guildford office, post-team-split. Both the Art Lead and the Programming Lead gave me feedback that indicated I had been too keen to jump in and answer questions on the team Discord when on many occaisions they felt that as discipline leads they should have been the right person to answer those questions that were within their disciplines. In my defence the shortened time scale of the production lead me to having some anxiety over any and all delays to progress -- I of course apologised for 'stepping on their toes' in that way and they acknowledged that I didn't do it out of malice. In the end trust and understanding is what builds the strongest and most successful development teams, and it is something I'll be keeping an eye on in future productions.

I found especially gratifying that the whole entire team reassured me that there was nothing more I could have done to smooth over the design team conflicts of the early weeks of this production. I think their loyalty and kindness toward me spoke louder than the actual facts. In hindsight I could have been less forceful in my speech and been more open to being 'directed' on the design of the quest and the level. On the other hand I feel there was a good chance that those accommodations would have lead to even more role-creep and invited more bullying. I am however still resolved to keep an eye on my team management and communication style on future projects. Self awareness is everything. We can only hope to be good iterative designers of our own selves, no?

The game's menu page, as designed and realised by MB. I loved our use of the menu page as part of the mission onboarding, as I thought it was a nice little piece of efficient game tutorial/onboarding design. (the pink cannister is the McGuffin object of the demo's fetch quest)
